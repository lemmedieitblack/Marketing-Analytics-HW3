# Survival Analysis & CLV Findings Report

**Model Interpretation and Risk Factors**
Based on the AIC comparisons across our parametric models, the Log-Normal Accelerated Failure Time (AFT) model provided the best fit for our data (AIC: 2954.02). In an AFT model, positive coefficients indicate an increase in expected survival time (delaying churn), while negative coefficients indicate a faster time to churn. Our final model revealed that older age, longer tenure at a current address, and belonging to the E-service, Plus service, or Total service customer categories (compared to the baseline Basic service) act as protective factors that significantly extend customer lifetime. Conversely, having internet service (coef: -0.84), being unmarried (coef: -0.45), and having voice service (coef: -0.46) significantly accelerate the churn hazard. 

**Valuable Segments and Retention Strategy**
We define our most valuable segments as those yielding the highest average Customer Lifetime Value (CLV). Based on the model's discounted cash flow projections, the "Plus service" category is our most valuable segment with an average CLV of $463.73, followed closely by "E-service" at $441.91. Interestingly, subscribers *without* internet service demonstrate a higher average CLV ($442.95) than those with it ($350.22), reflecting the high churn risk associated with internet users in our model. To size our annual retention budget, we identified active subscribers with greater than a 50% conditional probability of churning within the next 12 months. This isolated 2 highly at-risk subscribers with a combined CLV of $248.54. Allocating 10% of this at-risk CLV to intervention costs, our annual retention budget for this sample is $24.85. Beyond direct financial incentives, I recommend implementing targeted retention campaigns aimed at our high-risk demographics (e.g., unmarried subscribers and standalone internet/voice users). Tactics should include proactive outreach and incentivizing them to upgrade into "Plus service" or "Total service" tiers, which our model proves will structurally increase their survival time.
